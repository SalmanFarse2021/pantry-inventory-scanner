UTA's pantry doesn't list what items they have available except for their Instagram and even then, it's a limited selection of weekly items. In addition, you have to book an appointment in advance to visit the pantry. 

 
So the problem is that you could book an appointment only to show up and not find what you're looking for, wasting your time. 

 
Our solution is basically making an inventory system for them and connecting it to a website that students can see what they have. The big caveat I want to mention first is that we likely need some type of approval for building and collaborating with them. However, if we can actually get them on board with us, then it's pretty straight foward and we can build it really quickly. 

 
The way it works is that the worker in the pantry scans the item they have in stock by taking a picture with their phone, and we use AI image classification to quickly grab the product’s name and all they have to do is put a quantity. The pantry itself isn’t that big of a place, and would take an afternoon to inventory everything. From there, everything that’s uploaded can be seen on the website. When a student comes and checks out, the worker just takes a picture of it and it handles the inventory update automatically. I’ve already looked into competitors and the closest one is a foodbank manager, but it’s paid and it doesn’t provide a frontend for users to see what they have in stock. It has a barcode scanner, but no AI image classification. We could provide both to make it more robust.
